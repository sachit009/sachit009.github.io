Project Neodymium: Automated Rare-Earth Magnet Demanufacturing Machine

Non-destructive recovery of high-grade sintered NdFeB permanent magnets from scrap EV and e-rickshaw motor rotors.

💡 The Ground Reality

Walking through motor scrap clusters in Lucknow, I watched scrap workers attack broken e-rickshaw motor rotors with chisels and hammers. Sintered Neodymium-Iron-Boron (NdFeB) magnets have a brutal 30–50 kg magnetic clamping pull and behave like brittle ceramic tiles: hammering shatters the rare metal into fine dust, while friction sparks spontaneous pyrophoric fires. High-grade Neodymium, Praseodymium, and Dysprosium—essential for India's EV transition—were being ruined by hand.

⚙️ The Hardware Solution

I engineered a physical demanufacturing machine combining thermal physics and mechanical automation. Using eddy-current induction heating, the rotor reaches its Curie Temperature (310°C–340°C), instantaneously demagnetizing the magnets while thermally softening the industrial epoxy glue. An automated Hall-Effect sensor array grades magnetic flux and spots fake motors, and a universal stainless-steel multi-finger press slides every magnet bar intact out of its slot in a 2.5-minute automated cycle—zero shatters, zero sparks.

Mesmerize Robot: Autonomous Line-Tracking & Maze Navigation Hardware

Custom 6x4" motherboard PCB integrating an 8-channel differential IR sensor array, dual TB6612FNG motor drivers, and real-time PID control.

⚡ The Engineering Challenge

Autonomous line-tracking and maze-solving robots frequently fail at competition speeds due to optical sensor noise, physical chassis vibration, and power rail instability caused by inductive motor back-EMF resetting the microcontroller. I designed Mesmerize Robot from scratch to solve these hardware hurdles with a dedicated, custom motherboard rather than fragile breadboard wiring.

🛠️ Hardware & Circuit Architecture

Engineered a 6x4 inch (152.4 x 101.6 mm) custom motherboard PCB in KiCad with 0.25mm signal traces and a 205mm isolated sensor harness. The board pairs a 30-pin ESP32 microcontroller running real-time PID loops with an 8-channel differential IR reflectance sensor array (`IR_S1` through `IR_S8`), a TB6612FNG dual MOSFET motor driver, and decoupled power filtering capacitors to completely isolate motor transients from the MCU logic. Added an on-board hardware calibration button and goal indicator LED.
