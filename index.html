Project Mesmerize: Autonomous tracking & optical guidance without the breadboard mess.

Custom 6x4" dual-layer motherboard PCB in KiCad. 8-channel differential optical IR array with dual TB6612FNG MOSFET drivers. Real-time PID control at 240MHz with zero motor noise resets.

💡 The Ground Reality & Challenge

Autonomous line-tracking and optical inspection vehicles frequently fail at high competition speeds: optical sensor glare on varying surfaces, inductive back-EMF from DC motors sending voltage spikes through logic rails that reset the microcontroller, and loose breadboard jumper wires. I engineered Project Mesmerize from scratch to solve these hardware bottlenecks with an industrial-grade, single-board architecture.

⚙️ Hardware & Circuit Architecture

Engineered a dedicated 6x4 inch (152.4 x 101.6 mm) dual-layer motherboard PCB in KiCad with 0.25mm signal routing and ground copper pours. The front features an 8-channel differential optical reflectance sensor array (`IR_S1` through `IR_S8`) with hardware comparator conditioning for sub-millimeter line edge detection. A 30-pin ESP32 microcontroller runs a closed-loop PID control algorithm at 240MHz, dynamically modulating PWM duty cycles to a TB6612FNG dual MOSFET driver. High-capacity electrolytic decoupling capacitors and Schottky flyback protection isolate 7.4V/12V motor transients from the sensitive 3.3V logic rail. Integrated tactile calibration and goal indicator LEDs for instant race-day tuning.
